The team

Built by students.

Mamaroneck Robotics is entirely student-led. From ideas to fully functional autonomous robots, every component is designed, manufactured, iterated, and coded by high school students. We work together to problem solve and deliver competition-ready robots on strict deadlines.

FIRST Tech Challenge

FTC 8490

Team 8490 competes in FIRST Tech Challenge, where 18"×18" robots face off in 2v2 alliance-based matches. Every robot is designed in Autodesk Fusion, built in the MHS Engineering Lab, and programmed in Java.

2025–26 season

DECODE

Peekskill Qualifier
2nd Place Innovate Award

Robot specs

Design goals
A compact robot built for fast iteration and reliable mechanisms. Heavy use of 3D printing let us prototype quickly and make custom parts tailored to our design.
Drivetrain
GoBilda Strafer chassis, 96mm mecanum wheels, and 5203 Yellow Jacket motors (19.2:1, 312 RPM).
Mechanisms
Dual flywheel launcher for consistent scoring, a rubber-band intake and transfer on geared servos, and a servo gate with an adjustable ramp for shooting from multiple distances.
Software
Java in Android Studio with custom drivetrain and mechanism classes. Automated controls reduce driver workload, while Road Runner, PID, encoders, and the IMU handle precise autonomous movement.

2024–25 season

INTO THE DEEP

Peekskill Qualifier

Robot specs

Design goals
A simple, consistent robot that favored efficiency over flashy, single-purpose attachments, refined through brainstorming, prototyping, and iteration.
Drivetrain
GoBilda U-Channel chassis, 96mm mecanum wheels, and 5203 Yellow Jacket motors (19.2:1, 312 RPM), laid out to leave room for the slides and arm.
Mechanisms
An arm on dual-axis linear slides with a parallel claw that handles blocks in many orientations, plus a linear slide and C-channel lift for hanging from the bar.
Software
Java in Android Studio. TeleOp used semi-autonomous positioning for intaking, bucket scoring, and specimen placement; Road Runner, PID, encoders, and an IMU drove autonomous.

MATE ROV Competition

Tigersharks ROV

The Tigersharks design, build, and pilot remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) to complete simulated underwater marine industrial missions, emphasizing waterproof electronics, buoyancy control, and live telemetry.

2025–26 season

MATE ROV

Pennsylvania Regional
2nd Place Overall
Best Technical Documentation

ROV specs

Design goals
A maneuverable, low-cost ROV built to maximize task completion, improving reliability and usability on previous designs through rapid prototyping.
Propulsion
Four Blue Robotics T200 thrusters, two vertical and two forward, for stability and easy control. Slight positive buoyancy brings the ROV to the surface if anything fails.
Attachments
A 3D-printed interlocking claw for PVC pipe and wire or plastic loops, using double helical gears to cut backlash and two servos at 2:1 for more torque. Also a mostly 3D-printed recoverable line subsystem.
Electronics
A compact acrylic enclosure holds a Raspberry Pi, Arduino, two cameras, ESCs, and buck converters. Thruster wires enter through watertight penetrators, parallel power conductors reduce voltage drop, and a strain-relief hub protects the tether.
Software
Ventgarden, written in Python, runs the topside control station. On the ROV, a Raspberry Pi streams the cameras while an Arduino handles PWM over RS-485, kept separate for reliability. Camera latency dropped from ~280 ms to ~90 ms with two live feeds.

2024–25 season

MATE ROV

Pennsylvania Regional
Creative Design Award

ROV specs

Design goals
A light, maneuverable, low-cost ROV focused on reliability and usability, building on previous designs with new tools to take on more tasks.
Propulsion
Four Blue Robotics T200 thrusters, two vertical and two forward, keeping the center of thrust close to the center of mass. Slight positive buoyancy for safety.
Attachments
A 3D-printed interlocking claw that grabs ½–2 in. PVC pipe and wire or plastic loops, with two servos at 2:1 and double helical gears to cut backlash. A peristaltic pump driven by a Blue Robotics T-100 takes water samples.
Electronics
An acrylic enclosure holds the Raspberry Pi, cameras, ESCs, buck converter, and Arduino. The five-line tether carries three power lines (run in parallel to limit voltage drop), Ethernet, and RS-485, with a strain-relief hub protecting the cables.
Software
Four custom libraries: Leviathan (central control), Snowfox (cameras), Spinefish (motors and servos), and Seaglide (float). Splitting them up improved robustness, cut network latency, and freed up bandwidth.
