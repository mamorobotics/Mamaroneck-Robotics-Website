SEASON_02

FTC // DECODE

YEAR 2025-2026 STATUS: COMPLETE

SYS_SPECS // 2025-2026

Design Goals
An evolving, unique robot design focused on efficient iteration, reliable mechanisms, and maximizing performance within a compact layout. We heavily utilized 3D printing for rapid prototyping and custom, application-specific parts, allowing us to quickly iterate and create designs tailored specifically to our robot.
Drivetrain
GoBilda Strafer chassis as our base, with 96mm mecanum wheels and 5203 planetary gear series Yellow Jacket motors using a 19.2:1 gear ratio and 312 RPM..
Mechanisms
Dual flywheel launcher for reliable and consistent scoring. Rubber-band intake and transfer system with geared servos, plus a servo-operated gate and adjustable ramp for controlled artifact handling and shooting at multiple distances.
Software
Java-based control using Android Studio, with custom drivetrain and mechanism classes. Automated controls reduce driver workload, while Road Runner, PID loops, motor encoders, and the IMU provide precise autonomous movement.

SEASON_01

FTC // INTO THE DEEP

YEAR 2024-2025 STATUS: COMPLETE

SYS_SPECS // 2024-2025

Design Goals
A simpler, more consistent robot focused on efficiency rather than flashy or highly specific attachments. We prioritized designs that were efficient and feasible within our constraints, using an iterative process of brainstorming, comparison, prototyping, and refinement.
Drivetrain
GoBilda U-Channel chassis with 96mm mecanum wheels and 5203 planetary gear series Yellow Jacket motors. The motors use a 19.2:1 gear ratio and run at 312 RPM, while the chassis layout was optimized to maximize space for the linear slides and arm.
Mechanisms
A robotic arm with dual-axis linear slides and a parallel claw designed to handle blocks in a variety of orientations. We also developed a bar-lift mechanism using a linear slide and C-channel, allowing the robot to lift and hang from the bar.
Software
Java-based software developed in Android Studio with a focus on ease of operation and versatility. TeleOp included semi-autonomous positioning for intaking, scoring buckets, and placing specimens, while Road Runner, PID loops, motor encoders, and an IMU were used for precise autonomous movement.

[SYSTEM.INIT] // COMPETITION_TIER: MATE_ROV

TIGERSHARKS ROV

The Tigersharks design, build, and pilot remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) to complete simulated underwater marine industrial missions, emphasizing waterproof electronics, buoyancy control, and live telemetry.

SEASON_02

MATE ROV

YEAR 2025-2026 STATUS: COMPLETE

SYS_SPECS // 2025-2026

Design Goals
A maneuverable and relatively inexpensive ROV designed to maximize task completion. Our main focus was improving reliability, usability, and efficiency through rapid prototyping and iterative design while also adding on to previous designs.
Propulsion
Four Blue Robotics T200 thrusters arranged with two facing upward and two facing forward. This layout was selected for stability, flexibility, balance, and ease of control. The ROV was designed to be slightly positively buoyant so it would return to the surface in the event of an onboard failure.
Attachments
A robust, 3D-printed interlocking claw designed to grasp PVC piping and loops of wire or plastic. Double helical gears reduce backlash and gear slippage, while two servos and a 2:1 gear ratio increase gripping torque. We also developed a versatile recoverable line subsystem, with the majority of its components 3D printed.
Electronics
A compact acrylic electronics enclosure houses a Raspberry Pi, Arduino, two cameras, ESCs, and buck converters. The system uses watertight penetrators, parallel power conductors to reduce voltage drop, and a strain-relief hub to protect the tether and electrical connections.
Software
A modular digital control system using Raspberry Pi, Arduino, and Python. Ventgarden serves as the central control system on the ground station, while camera transmission and PWM control are separated to improve reliability and flexibility. RS-485 is used for communication between the control station and ROV, and the system reduced camera latency from approximately 280 ms to 90 ms while supporting two camera feeds.

SEASON_01

MATE ROV

YEAR 2024-2025 STATUS: COMPLETE

SYS_SPECS // 2024-2025

Design Goals
A light, maneuverable, and relatively inexpensive ROV focused on increasing reliability, usability, and efficiency. We used rapid prototyping and iterative design to expand on previous designs while adding new tools to increase our potential workload.
Propulsion
Four Blue Robotics T200 thrusters arranged with two facing upward and two facing forward. This layout was selected for flexibility, balance, ease of control, and efficient use of space, while keeping the center of thrust close to the center of mass. The ROV was designed to be slightly positively buoyant for additional safety.
Attachments
A robust 3D-printed interlocking claw designed to grab PVC pipes between ½ and 2 inches as well as loops of wire or plastic. Two servos with a 2:1 gear ratio provide increased torque, while double helical gears reduce backlash and gear slippage. The ROV also features a peristaltic water-sample pump driven by a Blue Robotics T-100 motor.
Electronics
An acrylic enclosure houses the Raspberry Pi, cameras, ESCs, buck converter, and Arduino. The ROV uses five tether connections, including three power lines, Ethernet for data, and RS-485 communication. Parallel power conductors help limit voltage drop, while an onboard strain-relief hub protects the cables and structure.
Software
A modular control system built around four custom software libraries: Leviathan, Seaglide, Snowfox, and Spinefish. Leviathan acts as the central control system, Snowfox handles camera data, Spinefish manages motor and servo control, and Seaglide controls the float. The separated architecture improved robustness and error resistance while reducing network latency and increasing available bandwidth.
